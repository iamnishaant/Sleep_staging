# ADDING EOG VERSION — ERROR ANALYSIS

**Written 10 October 2026.** This is part of the EOG testing work, and it is
separate from the shipped pipeline.

**The question:** where does the current staging model (`student_N4kd`) go
wrong, especially on N1 and REM? And is the EOG experiment aimed at those
errors?

**What it touches:**
- It **only reads** cached validation outputs that already exist in
  `distillation/results/`.
- It writes **only inside this folder**.
- Nothing is trained, no existing file is changed, and the test split is
  never read.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `analyse_errors.py` | The analysis. Run `python analyse_errors.py` (CPU, under a minute) |
| `results/ERROR_ANALYSIS_REPORT.md` | All the tables, generated by the script |
| `results/error_analysis.json` | The same numbers, machine-readable |
| `figures/*.png` | Confusion matrix, errors by distance to a stage change, confidence of misses, EOG vs EEG |
| `test_analyse_errors.py` | 6 tests |

**It analyses the model as it actually ships:** calibrated probabilities, then
the REM minimum-run decoder. As a check, it reproduces the stored validation κ
exactly (0.6817) before analysing anything.

**Scope:** 31 validation nights from 16 subjects. These are findings about this
model on these nights, not universal facts.

---

## The five findings

### 1. Three nights drag the whole score down — the biggest single factor

| Validation | Accuracy | κ |
|---|---:|---:|
| All 31 nights | 76.2% | 0.682 |
| The same, without the 3 worst nights (7.8% of epochs) | **79.4%** | **0.724** |

- **All three are ST (telemetry) nights:** ST7201J0, ST7202J0 and ST7151J0. Two
  of them are the same person (subject ST720).
- **On these nights *every* model fails.** N4kd, its EEG-only twin, the
  EEG+EOG model and the 3-model teacher all score κ ≈ 0.06–0.16.
  - So it's not something one model got wrong. Something about these
    recordings, or this subject, is outside what all the models learned.
- **The cohort gap is wide in general:** SC nights score κ **0.729**; ST nights
  score **0.479**.
- **They also account for a big share of two common errors:** 23% of all
  N2→N3 errors and 31% of all N2→REM errors.

**This is diagnostic only. Nothing is excluded from any reported number.** It
does show where the largest gain is: making the model work on nights like
these is worth more than any architecture change discussed so far.

> **Update, same day — the cause was investigated** in
> `ADDING EOG VERSION - ST NIGHTS/`.
> - **Cause found: a preprocessing bug that misaligns labels and signal**,
>   confirmed against the raw Sleep-EDF files. It affects 13 of 31 validation
>   nights, including ST7201J0 and ST7202J0, and 7.1% of validation labels are
>   wrong.
> - Re-scored against corrected labels, N4kd's validation accuracy is
>   **79.2%** (κ 0.722, N1 F1 53.8, REM F1 80.2), not 76.2%.
> - This report's tables use the stored labels. Their error patterns partly
>   reflect the misalignment.
> - **ST7151J0** is a genuinely broken recording: 26% of its epochs are flat
>   or stuck.
> - **Amplitude is not the cause.**
>
> The original candidate list is kept below for the record.

**The cause is not identified yet.** Candidates include:
- **hardware:** different amplifiers or filtering in the ST recordings;
- **the drug:** the ST study used temazepam, which changes sleep-spindle and
  slow-wave activity;
- **the subject** themselves;
- **the expert scoring.**

### 2. The largest single confusion isn't N1 or REM — it's N2 predicted as N3

| Expert → predicted | Epochs |
|---|---:|
| **N2 → N3** | **1,659** (12% of all N2) |
| N1 → N2 | 1,050 |
| N2 → REM | 1,007 |
| N2 → N1 | 970 |

- **N3 is over-called:** its precision is only 59%, against 83% recall. The
  model predicts deep sleep too readily.
- **N2's recall (73.5%) is lower than REM's (81.9%).** Better N2/N3 separation
  would raise accuracy as much as REM work would.

### 3. N1: mostly boundary errors and near-misses — little room to gain

- **65% of N1 errors sit at the edge of an N1 run** (0–1 epochs from a stage
  change). N1 runs of 1 epoch are recognised only 39% of the time; runs of 10+
  epochs, 58%.
- **When N1 is missed, N1 was the model's second choice 90% of the time.**
  These are borderline calls, not blind spots.
- **Where the misses go:** 53% to N2, 28% to W. N1 after REM is missed as REM
  192 times.

This is what you'd expect from a stage that **expert scorers themselves agree
on only ~63% of the time.** The labels are uncertain exactly where the model
is uncertain. Expect small N1 gains from any method.

### 4. REM: errors in the middle of REM periods, made confidently — a real signal gap

- **Only 35% of REM errors are at edges.** Most are inside REM periods.
- **When REM is missed, REM was the second choice only 35% of the time**, and
  it received just 0.19 probability on average. The model is confidently
  wrong: it really "sees" N2 there.
- **Where the misses go:** 59% to N2, 31% to N1. False REM calls come mostly
  from N2 (65%).
- **The errors are concentrated:** the worst 5 nights hold 49% of all REM
  errors, three of them ST.

A confident mistake in the middle of a period means **the information isn't in
the input.** That's the case where adding a signal, EOG, can help, and
smoothing or decoding can't.

### 5. Is EOG aimed at the right errors? Yes for REM and Wake; not for N1. And the old fusion damaged N2/N3

This compares, epoch by epoch, the existing EEG+EOG model (`N3mc_s42`) with its
EEG-only twin (`N2multiscale_fix`): same encoder, same training. Caveats: one
seed each, and the EOG model also added Pz-Oz.

| Expert stage | Net epochs gained with EOG | Reading |
|---|---:|---|
| **W** | **+272** (p ≈ 10⁻²⁹) | EOG helps wake: blinks and eye movements |
| **REM** | **+47** | Better on **21 of 31 nights** (Wilcoxon p = 0.011). Gains mostly *inside* REM periods (+29 at 2–4 epochs from an edge) — exactly finding 4's error type |
| N1 | −8 (p = 0.80) | **No effect** |
| **N2** | **−541** (p ≈ 10⁻³³) | **Damaged** |
| **N3** | **−123** (p ≈ 10⁻¹⁵) | **Damaged** |

**Of the REM epochs N4kd misses:**
- the EEG+EOG model gets **32%** right;
- the EEG-only twin gets 22%;
- the teacher gets 27%.

The equivalent figures for N1 are 25%, 18% and 15%. So EOG carries real REM
information that N4kd lacks.

**This explains the old "EOG = noise" result.** Real REM and wake gains were
cancelled by N2/N3 losses from fusing EOG into the EEG filters (plus Pz-Oz).
The new design in `ADDING EOG VERSION/` targets exactly this:
- the gate starts closed, so EOG can't enter where EEG is already right;
- EOG gets its own encoder;
- Pz-Oz is dropped.

## What this changes in the plan

1. **EOG run: keep going, with a sharper expectation.**
   - **Expect:** REM and Wake to improve.
   - **Don't expect:** much change in N1.
   - **The critical check:** that N2 and N3 don't drop. The trainer's final
     "EOG switched off" comparison shows this stage by stage, so read it per
     stage, not just overall κ.
2. **New top priority: investigate the ST nights.** The next CPU analysis
   should compare the EEG signals of ST and SC recordings:
   - amplitude and frequency spectrum, especially sigma (spindles) and delta;
   - signal quality on ST720 and ST715.

   If it's a recording or amplitude difference, a targeted fix (per-cohort
   normalisation, or domain-adaptive training) could be worth several points.
3. **N3 over-calling** (N2 → N3) is the largest single error. Worth a look at
   delta-band amplitude per recording; slow-wave amplitude falls with age.
4. **Don't spend more effort on decoding or N1 thresholds.**
   - Decoding: already tested on N4kd, worth about +0.002 κ.
   - N1 thresholds: tested, at most +0.0086 F1.

The remaining errors come from the input and the recordings, not from
post-processing.
